# Craft and distribution

Craft slice. Retrieved 31 Oct 2026.

Status: ISSUES. Official ranking pages name watch-choice and continued watching as distribution inputs, but TikTok’s live pages timed out, length studies disagree with the new three-minute caps, and the caption study’s own tests were mostly not significant.

## Findings

### Hook
TikTok Creator Academy, storytelling article (page undated; search extract 31 Oct 2026, full fetch timed out). Method: in the first few seconds, and it also says the first 5 seconds, state a question that is only answered if the viewer stays, open on the finished result, name a time box (“in 2 mins”), use “best saved for last” language, or start at the climax and backtrack. Pair that with on-screen text that states the theme. Claimed result: viewers otherwise swipe; these openings are meant to stop the swipe and create a reason to stay. No lift is measured.

Instagram for Creators, “Helping creators of all sizes break through,” 26 Feb 2025. Method: a hook in the first 3 seconds that stands alone for people who do not follow you, then watch View Rate, defined as the share who keep watching after those 3 seconds. Claimed result: non-followers are the growth path; the 3-second continuation rate is the diagnostic, not a published benchmark.

YouTube Help, “Search & discovery tips” for Shorts (fetched 31 Oct 2026; page undated). Method: the first ranking question is whether a viewer chooses to watch, ignores the Short, or taps “not interested.” Claimed result: “% of viewers who chose to view” is a ranking signal, separate from how long they then stay. YouTube says it does not favor a format.

### Retention
TikTok Creator Academy, recommendation-system article (undated; search extract). Method: cut lulls and use quick cuts. Claimed result: longer watch time is treated as a signal of compelling content for the For You feed. The same article says users spend 96% of their time in that feed. No per-video lift is given.

TikTok Creator Academy, analytics article (undated; search extract). Method: read retention rate (share of the video watched), find the drop-off, average watch time, and “watched full video.” Claimed result: the finish rate moves with total length, so a high completion rate is not comparable across durations. A different Academy article says some content metrics cover only the last 7 days, while the analytics article describes 7-, 28-, 60-, and 365-day ranges. Those windows were not reconciled.

TikTok Creator Academy, “Creating high-quality videos” (undated; search extract; full fetch timed out). Claimed result: “high-quality” creators get 72% more accumulated watch time per view than other creators, 42% more than other accounts with 10,000+ followers, more than 40 times the follower growth, and 3 times the new followers per video. “High-quality” is not defined as a reproducible edit, so this is a platform marketing claim, not a craft recipe.

Instagram, 26 Feb 2025. Ranking inputs named: watch time, retention, shares, likes, and comments, plus similar-audience matching. A Reel is shown to a small non-follower group first; wider distribution follows if that group responds. Also claimed: Instagram video time grew by double-digit percentages year over year in Q4 2024, and people reshare Reels more than 4.5 billion times a day across Instagram and Facebook.

YouTube Shorts help, fetched 31 Oct 2026. After the view-choice signal, ranking uses average view duration and average percent viewed, then likes and post-watch surveys. Sounds the viewer has already engaged with are used to suggest other Shorts. Shorts performance does not reduce long-form recommendations.

Sheynin, *The Young Researcher* 9(1), 2025, videos coded 23 Dec 2024. On a one-day For You sample of 100 TikToks, only music had a significant association with a custom engagement score. Length, pacing, text overlay, and format did not (ANOVA, p > 0.05). In a survey of 438 US teens aged 14–18, the clip built from the higher-scoring traits was called more engaging by 69.2% and better at holding attention by 74.9%. Of teens who preferred that clip, 81.7% credited fast pacing. This is a student journal, one coder, and the abstract is stronger than the tests.

### Length
YouTube blog, Todd Sherman, 3 Oct 2024, and YouTube Help on three-minute Shorts (fetched via search, 31 Oct 2026). From 15 Oct 2024, square or vertical uploads up to 3 minutes are Shorts. Claimed result: more time to tell a story. Help also says most Audio Library songs can be used for up to 90 seconds inside a 3-minute Short; some tracks are capped at 60 or 30 seconds. A Short over 1 minute with any active copyright claim is blocked globally. That is a distribution kill, not a retention tip.

Instagram for Creators, 21 Jan 2025 (search extract; page not fully fetched). The post announces 3-minute Reels. About Instagram’s Reels page separately says recording and editing up to 20 minutes. Those two limits were not reconciled. Do not treat either number as an optimum.

Reichstein and Dost, Marketing Trends Congress, 2025. In a survey experiment (310 ratings; length, music, and advertising varied), self-rated engagement followed an inverted U, peaking at 34.69 seconds (90% interval 33.21–36.14). Music moved the peak to 44.09 seconds and raised engagement. Advertising moved it to 19.82 seconds and lowered engagement. This is intended engagement, not platform watch time.

Sapiens Global reanalysis of 48 geoscience TikToks posted Oct 2021–Feb 2022 (publication date not confirmed as 2025 beyond the search record). Median length 54.96 seconds, average percent watched 41.73%, completion 18.46%. Longer videos had lower retention (Spearman ρ = −0.473). Retention (ρ = 0.328) and completion (ρ = 0.390) were positively associated with a composite engagement rate. One account, observational, not causal.

TikTok’s own analytics note that finish rate fluctuates with length, which matches the tension above: absolute watch time and percent watched pull in different directions as videos get longer.

### Captions
TikTok Creator Academy, storytelling article (undated; search extract). Method: burned-in captions so people can follow, plus sticker text to introduce the next point, plus a clear voiceover. Claimed result: pacing stays high enough that people stay. No numeric lift.

YouTube Help, clips and Shorts tool (search extract). Where available, the tool burns auto-captions from the source transcript and lets the creator restyle or disable them. That is a product behavior, not a retention result.

Sheynin 2025. “Present text overlay” had a higher descriptive engagement score than no overlay, and was common in the sample, but the ANOVA was not significant. Among teens who preferred the weaker clip, 39.7% still named on-screen text as what engaged them. Treat on-screen text as a test factor, not a proven lift.

A separate 2025 European Marketing Academy paper (Reichstein, Dost, Brusch, and Brusch; 75,000 posts) is about the length of the post caption under the video, not burned-in words. It reports an S-shaped relationship with engagement. It does not belong in a burned-in-caption test.

### Cadence
YouTube Shorts help, fetched 31 Oct 2026. “There is no minimum posting cadence required for your videos to perform well.” The suggested method is to match audience and the creator’s pace. Subscribed viewers see recent uploads on the Subscriptions tab, so recency matters there even if the Shorts feed is performance-ranked.

Instagram, 26 Feb 2025. “Post regularly,” with no count. Claimed mechanism: each Reel can be shown to non-followers, so another post is another draw. The same post describes Trial Reels: publish first to non-followers, then decide whether followers see it. That matches an approve-before-wide-release workflow.

Hashtags, Instagram, 18 Dec 2025. The Verge reports Adam Mosseri capping hashtags at five per post: a few specific tags “perform better than a long list of generic ones,” tags help search, and they “don’t increase your reach.” Social Media Today and Hindustan Times quote Instagram the same day: the caption limit moves gradually to five; fewer targeted tags can improve performance and the viewing experience; generic tags such as #reels and #explore do not help Explore and can hurt. Instagram Help still describes adding hashtags in the caption or comments and was truncated before a numeric cap, so the help page may be stale.

YouTube Shorts help. Using a sound from the Audio Library raises the chance of appearing on that sound’s page. Hashtag count is not specified. TikTok’s storytelling article says trending sounds make videos more discoverable, and Creator Search Insights can supply topics, titles, and hashtags. No count or lift is given.

### Cross-posting
Instagram for Creators, 30 Apr 2024. Recommendation eligibility is illustrated with content that is original, follows the rules, and has no visible watermarks. When Instagram finds identical copies and is confident from audio and visual signals, it recommends only the original, and only while that original is relatively new. A material change — meme, parody, new voiceover, or remix — is not replaced. Accounts that repeatedly repost other people’s content without a material change are removed from recommendations. Follower feeds are unaffected.

The same watermark rule is older and more explicit in The Verge, 9 Feb 2021: recycled video with a watermark, logo, border, or mostly covered by text is recommended less often to non-followers, because surveys found that recycled or blurry Reels were less satisfying. The 2024 page still lists “no visible watermarks,” so the 2021 mechanism was not retired in the later post.

Instagram, 21 Jan 2025. The Edits app is described as able to export without a watermark. That is a production constraint for a clean master, not a measured reach gain.

Instagram for Creators, “rewarding original creators,” search extract dated in secondary coverage as 30 Apr 2026 (page not fully fetched). The 2024 Reel originality rules extend to photos and carousels. Accounts that primarily post unoriginal photos, carousels, or Reels lose recommendation eligibility and can regain it when a significant share of recent posts is original over a rolling 30 days. A secondary writeup says a border, watermark, subtitles, or a caption credit does not make someone else’s footage original. That sentence was not confirmed on the primary page.

YouTube, Digiday, early Aug 2021, quoting a YouTube spokesperson about the Shorts Fund. A TikTok watermark made a video ineligible for that fund. The same video, uploaded without the watermark, could qualify even if it had already been on TikTok. Exclusivity was not required. This is a 2021 fund rule. It was not reconfirmed against 2026 Shorts monetization or recommendation docs.

TikTok’s integrity-and-authenticity guidelines page timed out. Do not treat secondhand paraphrases of “another app’s watermark is ineligible for For You” as a verified primary quote.

## Conflicts
Length. YouTube (15 Oct 2024) and Instagram (21 Jan 2025) raised the product cap to about 3 minutes. Reichstein and Dost (2025) peak at about 35 seconds of stated engagement, or 44 with music. The geoscience reanalysis finds longer videos retain a smaller share. TikTok says finish rate moves with length and that longer watch time helps distribution. Absolute seconds and percent watched are different outcomes.

Captions. TikTok recommends burned-in captions with no number. Sheynin’s abstract endorses text overlay; the ANOVA does not. The EMAC S-curve is about the written post caption, a different object.

Hashtags. Mosseri, 18 Dec 2025, says specific tags help search and do not increase reach, while Instagram’s quoted announcement says fewer targeted tags can improve performance. Both reject long generic lists. They do not agree on whether tags change distribution beyond search.

Cadence. YouTube says no minimum is required. Instagram says post regularly because each Reel is a new non-follower draw. Neither gives a frequency that beat another frequency.

Watermarks versus your own cross-post. Instagram’s identical-copy rule is about copies on Instagram. No fetched official page says posting your own clean file to TikTok, Reels, and Shorts on the same day is penalized. The penalty that is documented is another app’s visible watermark, plus reposting someone else’s footage without a material change.

“High-quality” watch-time gaps on TikTok (72%, 42%, 40×, 3×) are not tied to a defined edit, so they should not be used as an experiment contrast.

## Inferences
Inference. A useful experiment row can vary hook type, hook window, length, on-screen text, cut density, audio source, hashtag count and specificity, cadence, and whether the file carries another app’s watermark. Hold the spoken script constant when the contrast is visual or distributional.

Inference. Columns worth encoding: `hook_type` (question, end-result tease, time box, saved-for-last, cold-open climax), `hook_window_sec` (3 or 5), `length_sec`, `burned_in_caption` (none, speech transcript, topic cards), `cut_density`, `audio` (voice only, voice plus trending sound, music only), `hashtag_count`, `hashtag_specificity`, `watermark` (none versus another app), `cadence_per_week`, `platform`, and `trial_before_followers` on Instagram. Outcomes: chose-to-view or 3-second view rate, average watch time, average percent viewed, completion, shares, and likes. Completion should be compared only inside a length band.

Inference. Because YouTube ranks both duration and percent viewed, a longer cut can win on seconds and lose on percent at the same time. Log both.

Inference. Instagram’s identical-audio-and-picture rule is a reason to upload the clean master once per platform, not to download a published TikTok and re-upload that file.

Inference. Trial Reels are the closest official match to “approve, then release”: non-followers see it first, and follower distribution is a second decision.
