# Faceless accounts

Faceless slice. Retrieved 31 Oct 2026.

Status: ISSUES. Named operators and view counts are sourced, but every dollar figure below is an operator or company claim, and Tim Danilov’s course pages contradict his interview.

## Findings

### Mathis Bolt — Bolt Motivation
YouTube Shorts, TikTok, and Instagram. Niche is motivational stories of people overcoming adversity, which he distinguishes from “somebody yelling at you to start doing push-ups.” Format is about one minute: other people’s footage recut into a story, glowing motion-blur captions, a small bottom watermark, and an AI voiceover. He said he does not add a face cam. On the 1of10 Podcast (8 May 2026) he described the story test he uses: a hero who wants something, something in the way, and what life looks like if they fail.

He posts the same video to YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram, and described a stretch of posting every day or two, sometimes after about 10 hours on one video. Early quote-style clips, he said, did better on Instagram; story videos did better on YouTube. He said TikTok pushes a video for about two or three days and then it dies, while old YouTube Shorts keep getting views.

Call to action he described: the line “want to edit like this link in bio” above his watermark, tied to an editing cheat sheet. He said that pop-up format was later reused for a Cali AI sponsorship.

Monetization he described: YouTube pays “by far” the best; a TikTok CPM sponsorship; the cheat sheet; the Cali AI deal. All of these are his claims.

Figures, all his claims:
- Undated YouTube interview, earlier than May 2026 (he had graduated two weeks prior; on the May podcast, graduation was “a few months ago”): about 250 million views a month across platforms, of which he estimated about 150 million from new videos. YouTube payout, in Canadian dollars per 1,000 views: about 8 cents when he started, then 15, then 20, and “usually” about 30 cents at the time of that interview. He said that if he stopped posting, the old catalog would probably still make about $300 a day for the next year. Team at that interview: “just me.” The transcript drops the number when he is asked his best month (“I’ve made in a single month”).
- 1of10 Podcast, 8 May 2026: age 18, about 2.3 million YouTube subscribers, “over three billion views” on short-form in two years. “The highest monthly revenue I’ve had has been about 40 to 50K,” which he attributed to YouTube Shorts. An early TikTok CPM deal at about 15 cents per thousand views produced about 10 million views in a week and about $3,000 to $4,000. Team by then: one clip finder, two editors (one in training), one developer.

The 1of10 show also sells its own software. The revenue lines are still the guest speaking, not a page selling his course.

### Tim Danalov / Tim Danilov — Art of War
YouTube Shorts channel he calls Art of War. The auto-caption also hears “Ark of War.” The course site spells his name Danilov; the interview caption spells it Danalov. Faceless, and in the same interview he describes 3D animation as the hard, expensive part of the work. Niche on the course site is packaged as a branded faceless channel; the interview itself is about a Shorts channel that used 3D animation.

In the YouTube interview “How He Made $250,000 On A Faceless Channel In 1 YEAR” (host: Travis), he said his first video went up on 27 January, “almost exactly one year” before the interview, and that they posted about once every two days. The first videos “stuck at like 15k views” until the seventh.

The host, looking at screenshots the guest was showing, said “for the year, $260,000 in revenue from that channel, from a shorts channel.” The video title says $250,000. That is the guest’s analytics as read aloud, not an outside audit. The interview does not print the year. His course page says the channel launched in January 2025, which would place this interview about a year later if it is the same upload. That date link is an inference.

He also said that at about 80 to 100 million views a month he was making about $6,000 to $7,000 a month while paying a freelancer about $4,000 to $6,000 for 15 videos a month, so he was not keeping a profit, and that he needed about 100 to 120 million views a month before he did. He said he sold part of the channel to an investor who runs a large agency, then moved from one-off Upwork edits to full-time staff.

### Zack D. Films
YouTube Shorts. Tubefilter, using GospelStats, is the independent count.

Sam Gutelle, Tubefilter, 19 September 2023, quoted the channel’s own line, “a collection of things I find interesting,” and described narration in the style of other Shorts storytellers. The example he gave was a Short on why McDonald’s McFlurry spoon looks the way it does. For the week of 11–17 September 2023 the channel had 123,431,885 views, up 18% from the week before, 5.45 million subscribers, and 5,112,190,468 lifetime views. The article said the main channel had been active for about two years.

Sam Gutelle, Tubefilter, 10 February 2025: Zack D. Films was the most-viewed U.S. YouTube channel that week, with 549.2 million weekly views, and 19.3 million subscribers all-time. The piece calls the channel a “content aggregator.” That label conflicts with the 2023 description and with animator Chris Reilly’s portfolio, which says he made 100-plus original 3D shorts for the channel (history, science, medicine) and that those videos passed 3 billion views combined, for a channel “close to 20 million subscribers.” The portfolio page has no date.

No posting cadence, hook script, call to action, or revenue figure from Zack or from YouTube turned up in these sources. Sites that print a monthly ad income are models, and they do not agree with each other. They are not reported here as earnings.

### TheSoul Publishing — 5-Minute Crafts, Bright Side, 123 GO!
Limassol studio, founded 2016, still operating in 2024 and 2025. Faceless DIY and life-hack video, distributed as short-form on social platforms and also cut into long compilations. Cyprus Business News, 23 August 2024, reported the company’s own announcement that its short-form portfolio had passed 100 billion views in 2024 so far, and that the company “reaches over 2 billion followers.” Those totals are the portfolio, not 5-Minute Crafts alone. CBN calls 5-Minute Crafts the top DIY digital brand and a top YouTube and Facebook page.

Business Insider, March 2025, quoted Victor Potrel, then senior vice president of partnerships and creator services: they package existing videos into hour-long TV compilations by theme, including home decor, and “You don’t need to radically change the way that you produce content. Mostly it’s been about packaging.” The company said its share of global views on connected TVs was 24% in 2024, up from 17% in 2022, and watch time on TVs was 41%, up from 28%.

Digiday, quoting Potrel when he was VP of platform partnerships, described the money as the platform ad products on Facebook, YouTube, and Snapchat (pre-roll, mid-roll, post-roll, revenue share). He would not give the dollar amount. That article is from the 2020 results cycle, so it is only a description of the model, not a 2024–2026 revenue figure. No 2024–2026 dollar revenue for 5-Minute Crafts or TheSoul was in the pages opened here.

Cadence, hook lines, and a consumer call to action were not in these sources.

### Dr. Filou / Dr. Philou
TikTok and YouTube, five faceless channels, of which he said two are more carefully made. The best one, which the interview spells both Dr.Filou and Dr.Philou, is business advice. He described burned-in subtitles as the piece he sweats: about an hour per short in CapCut before, then five shorts in two hours with Submagic, including emoji and keyword sound effects. He said three of the channels are in TikTok’s monetization program.

His claims, in a Submagic interview: Dr. Philou had “over 90,000 subscribers”; the first month of TikTok monetization was about €300; the first month of affiliate commissions on that channel was about €800. The interview body describes faceless advice videos and captions. It does not describe a synthetic person on screen. The page has no visible publication date, so these figures cannot be pinned to 2024–2026. It is a vendor interview for a captions product. His advice in the interview was to pick a niche that can be posted consistently and to watch what competitors’ videos do.

## Patterns

YouTube Shorts ad payouts are the revenue these operators actually quantify, and the rate is cents per thousand views. Mathis Bolt said YouTube pays best and that his YouTube rate moved from about 8 cents to about 30 cents Canadian per 1,000 views. Tim Danalov said about $6,000 to $7,000 in a month when a channel was doing about 80 million views, which did not cover the editor.

Both of those operators posted on a tight cadence. Bolt described every day or two. Danalov said Art of War launched at once every two days.

Production leaves the operator once the channel is working. Bolt, by 8 May 2026, had a clip finder and editors. Danalov moved from a single freelancer to full-time staff after the freelance 3D videos ate the ad revenue. Zack D. Films has outside 3D work documented by Chris Reilly.

The same short is pushed through more than one shelf. Bolt put every video on YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram from the start. TheSoul runs one production style across a portfolio of brands and platforms, then re-packages it for TV.

## Conflicts

Tim Danalov’s interview title says $250,000 in a year. The host, reading the on-screen analytics, says $260,000. His course pages (theartofyt.com and the company-written Trustpilot description) say a $700,000-a-year portfolio, 5 billion views in one version and 6 billion in another, and for Art of War specifically 1.6 million followers, 4 billion views, and over $300,000 in AdSense in 400 days. Those pages also name Wholesome Wendy and Zeck Felms, which this interview did not document. Treat the course numbers as sales claims.

Zack D. Films is an original narrated explainer in Tubefilter’s September 2023 piece and in Chris Reilly’s portfolio, and a “content aggregator” in Tubefilter’s February 2025 piece. Earnoscope, vidIQ, and a third-party YouTube calculation all publish different ad-income estimates. None of those is Zack’s or YouTube’s number.

TheSoul has no disclosed 2024–2026 profit in the sources opened here. A Lawfare article cites Nox Influencer ranges for Bright Side and 5-Minute Crafts. A LinkedIn résumé claims creator-distribution revenue under a TheSoul team went from $5.5 million in 2024 to $11.8 million in 2025. That résumé is about partner and talent distribution, not the ad revenue of 5-Minute Crafts. Digiday’s “45% revenue increase” is the 2020 comparison and has no base dollar figure.

Mathis Bolt’s team size differs across interviews in a way that fits the dates: “just me” in the earlier YouTube interview, and a four-person production group on 8 May 2026. The best-month dollar amount is missing from the earlier transcript and is $40,000 to $50,000 on the May podcast.

Dr. Filou and Dr. Philou are the same interview’s two spellings. The page is undated.

## Inferences

Inference: $6,000 to $7,000 on about 80 million views is roughly 7.5 to 9 cents per thousand views. That is arithmetic on Danalov’s interview figures, not a rate he stated.

Inference: if Art of War’s first upload was 27 January 2025, as the course page says, the “one year later” interview is early 2026. The interview recording itself does not state the year.

Inference: Dr. Philou’s “over 90,000 subscribers” is TikTok, because the next sentence is the 10,000-subscriber TikTok monetization threshold. He does not say the platform in that clause.

Inference: a channel with Zack D. Films’ public view counts is in a position to collect YouTube ad revenue. That is not a measured profit, and it leaves out animation cost, which is the gap Danalov said made a similar 3D Shorts channel unprofitable at 80 million views a month.

Inference: Bolt’s “probably $300 per day” is a hypothetical about stopping posting, not a reported month of income.
