# Money paths and tests

Money slice. Retrieved 31 Oct 2026.

Status: ISSUES. Official help pages document TikTok Creator Rewards, YouTube Shorts revenue share, and invite-only Instagram bonuses, but TikTok’s own pages disagree on the cash-out floor, and this pass found no method-backed short-video affiliate conversion rate.

## Findings

### Platform payouts

**TikTok.** The program named on TikTok’s 2026 Creator Academy and help pages is the **Creator Rewards Program**, which those pages contrast with the older Creator Fund. This pass did not find a live 2026 page still titled Creativity Program. The Academy eligibility article was marked last updated August 18, 2026.

To join, a creator must be 18 or older, use a personal account in good standing, be based in and registered in the United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, South Korea, France, Mexico, or Brazil, and have at least 10,000 followers and 100,000 video views in the last 30 days. Business accounts and accounts tied to organizations or to political or government bodies do not qualify. A business account switched to personal can apply only after 30 days. The account must be public.

A video earns only if it is original, high quality, longer than one minute, and reaches at least 1,000 qualified For You views. Duets, Stitches, Photo Mode, and ads, paid promotions, or sponsored content do not qualify. Qualified views are unique For You views with at least five seconds watched. They exclude “not interested” taps, the creator’s own views, paid, promoted, fraudulent, and artificial views. Search views need at least 30 seconds. Views count only from the eight program countries. Only videos posted after approval earn. TikTok says it may cap rewards monthly or annually. The US terms say reward criteria are at TikTok’s discretion.

Rewards are Standard Reward plus Additional Reward. Standard Reward uses qualified views and an RPM that TikTok says depends on watch time and finish rate, search traffic, viewer and creator location, engagement, and ad value. Additional Reward is described as well-crafted (1080p or higher, clear, polished, over one minute), engaging, and specialized. **TikTok does not publish a numeric RPM.** Estimated rewards can change after fees, tax, and other adjustments. Payment is described as the 15th of the month after the earning month, through PayPal via Hyperwallet.

The cash-out floor is not consistent across TikTok’s own pages. See Conflicts. Do not treat either $10 or $50 as the lab’s expected minimum until the operator’s own dashboard and regional terms are read.

Ineligible factors that matter for this lab: unoriginal or minimally edited content; split screens, meaningless reactions, low-quality images, or slide videos; a profile that contains personal or business contact information; clickbait aimed at followers, likes, or ad clicks; five video violations in 30 days; five account violations, which TikTok says make the account permanently ineligible.

**YouTube Shorts.** Current YouTube Partner Program entry, still in force on October 31, 2026, is: live where the program is available, no active Community Guidelines strikes, 2-step verification, advanced features, and an AdSense for YouTube account, plus either 1,000 subscribers and 4,000 qualified public watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers and 10 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts Feed watch hours do not count toward the 4,000 hours. Qualified Shorts views are public Shorts in the Shorts Feed. Private, unlisted, deleted, ad-campaign, and Image Post views do not count. This pass did not open the country list or the AdSense cash-out threshold page, so those minimums are not stated here.

Shorts ad revenue requires accepting the Shorts Monetization Module. Views before acceptance do not earn. Revenue comes from ads between videos in the Shorts Feed, separate from long-form Watch Page ads. YouTube’s stated steps: pool that ad revenue; allocate a Creator Pool by engaged views and music use; pay the creator 45% of their allocated share. A Short with one music track puts half of its associated revenue in the Creator Pool and half toward music licensing. Two tracks put one third in the Creator Pool. The 45% applies after that allocation, whether or not music was used. Only advertiser-friendly views count. Non-original Shorts, fake views, and views that break advertiser-friendly rules are ineligible. YouTube does not publish an RPM.

Starting February 1, 2027, a channel must keep 10 million qualified Shorts views in the prior 90 days to earn that month from the Shorts Creator Pool and related YouTube Premium revenue. Missing that does not remove the channel from the Partner Program and does not stop other Partner Program earnings. New applicants from that date need 1,000 subscribers and either 8,000 qualified watch hours in 365 days or 20 million qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Creators already in the program keep their entry status. Fan funding, Creator Partnerships, and Shopping stay at 500 subscribers and either 3,000 watch hours in a year or 3 million Shorts views in 90 days. If an advertiser targets an ad to five or fewer channels, YouTube says eligible creators can earn a direct 45% of those placements on top of the pool. Updated terms must be accepted by January 31, 2027, or earning from the related features stops on February 1, 2027. Not signing does not remove Partner Program status.

YouTube also says that, based on 2026 performance, creators earn more per user from Premium than from ads. That is YouTube’s qualitative statement, not a Shorts RPM.

**Instagram.** There is no general, always-on Reels revenue share with a published rate or a public eligibility checklist comparable to YouTube’s.

What Instagram does publish is an **invite-only, limited-time bonus**. The help center says invitations go to select creators located in, and with tax status in, Japan, South Korea, or the United States, age 18 or older, with a professional account, who follow Partner Monetization Policies and Content Monetization Policies. The Reels bonus also requires at least 5 million views in the previous three consecutive calendar months and excludes aggregator accounts. Not every eligible creator is invited. A separate Instagram creators page says the Reels bonus is only available in the United States and South Korea. See Conflicts.

Earning rules on the help page: public Reels or posts; original content only for the Reels bonus; each piece can earn for up to 28 days; the most recent 150 pieces are eligible in each two-week period. Branded content, boosted content, and collaborative posts are excluded. Qualified Reel views exclude repeat views. Viewers must be logged in. Views from the creator’s own profile do not count. Instagram says it adjusts the estimate for audience age and country and for engagement. The dashboard can show an average RPM, defined as approximate earnings divided by qualified views, times 1,000. **No public RPM is stated.** A payout account is required. A bonus may require a minimum milestone shown on that creator’s bonus screen; missing it can forfeit earnings. The amount is not published as a global minimum.

Meta’s business help page says creators who meet eligibility requirements and agree to terms can receive a share of revenue from ads on Instagram Reels, and that ads may also run on Reels from creators who are not paid. It does not publish the share or the eligibility list. Instagram’s monetization-tools list names Badges, Bonuses, Creator Marketplace, Gifts, and Subscriptions. It does not name a general Reels ad-revenue product. A separate help page says monetizing ads in the profile feed and Reels is a test for a select group, and that earnings may change because the feature is still being refined. A May 2023 Meta news post said an Instagram test would pay a small group in select markets by plays, not by the ads’ own revenue. That post is three years old.

### Affiliate and own offer

How an offer is attached, from platform rules rather than from a conversion study:

- TikTok Creator Rewards and a paid or sponsored video are on a collision course. The Academy says ads, paid promotions, and sponsored content do not qualify for rewards, and that personal or business contact information on the profile can make the account ineligible, as can content designed to draw advertisement clicks. A trackable affiliate link in the bio is the usual attachment, and it is also the thing those rules flag.
- Instagram bonuses exclude branded content. A disclosed affiliate Reel can still get views and, if the operator’s own link is clicked, clicks. It does not earn the bonus.
- YouTube Shorts revenue is not described as blocked by an affiliate link. It is blocked for non-original and non-advertiser-friendly views. Disclosure of realistic synthetic media is a separate requirement, below.

What converts versus what only gets views: **this pass found no 2025–2026 study with a stated method that measures short-video clicks or purchases by placement** (bio, redirect, comment, on-screen CTA).

Figures that showed up, and that the lab should not treat as expected rates:

- A February 2026 Reddit post claims 2% click-through for “link in bio” and 18% for comment-to-DM, on 50 sales posts. Unsourced claim. No denominator, no offer, no platform split, no holdout.
- Manychat’s case study of DIY Eule says one Instagram Reel’s comment keyword sent a DM carousel of affiliate links, ran 1,376 automations, and produced over €28,000 in Amazon sales and €2,000 in commissions. Vendor case study. One viral Reel. Not a rate.
- ChitChatBot.ai case studies claim link-in-bio or landing-page paths under 1% into a newsletter, and comment-to-DM at 22% and 14% from commenter to subscriber. Vendor marketing. Email signup, not a purchase. Unsourced as a rate the lab should expect.
- A 2018 arXiv study of YouTube and Pinterest found affiliate URLs correlated with higher views and likes, and that only about 10% of affiliate content carried a disclosure (about 7% “affiliate link” wording and about 2% longer explanations on YouTube). That is correlation on long-form and pins from 2018. It does not measure short-video click or purchase conversion. Selection bias is likely: popular videos are more likely to carry links.

The usable distinction is policy, not a conversion table. Views can accrue on a TikTok or Instagram video that is ineligible for the platform payout because it is sponsored, branded, or an ad click. Clicks only exist if the operator’s own redirect records them. Platform view counts are not that record.

### How to test

This pass found **no interview or write-up from a short-form operator** that publishes a pre-registered sample size, a one-factor protocol, or a decision window with a method. The methods below are from web experimentation, applied here by inference.

Adobe Target says the sample size has to be chosen before a fixed-horizon A/B test, and that watching until a result looks significant underestimates the confidence interval and raises the false-positive rate, because the test stops on the lucky look and continues on the unlucky ones. Evan Miller’s sequential method is the published alternative to peeking: pick N in advance, stop and call a winner only if the success gap hits \(2\sqrt{N}\), or stop with no winner when total successes hit N. Stopping before that boundary, or extending past N, invalidates the procedure. Kissmetrics states that if a test with no real effect is checked every day and stopped at the first p-value under 0.05, a “winner” appears about 30% of the time. That is their description of daily optional stopping, not a universal constant, and it was not re-run here.

Kissmetrics also states the multiple-comparisons problem: twenty metrics each tested at 5% give about a 64% chance that at least one looks significant when none is real. The fix they state is to name the primary metric before the test and treat other metrics as context.

No source in this pass gives a Shorts or Reels view count that is “enough.” A 7-day crown on views, with the hook, topic, length, and offer all changed, is the optional-stopping problem plus the multiple-comparisons problem at once.

### Failure modes

**Reused and template content.** YouTube’s channel monetization policy, updated July 15, 2025, renamed “repetitious content” to “inauthentic content” and said the change clarifies that repetitive or mass-produced content was already ineligible. Reused content is a separate rule: repurposing footage from YouTube or elsewhere without significant original commentary, substantive edits, or educational or entertainment value. Review is of the channel, not one video, and monetization can be removed from the whole channel. Permission from the original creator does not cure it. Allowed examples include criticism, rewritten dialogue, explained sports replays, reactions with commentary, and Shorts remixes that add original content. Not allowed in the inauthentic section: interchangeable template videos, the same storyline repeated, image slideshows or scrolling text with little narrative, and AI-generated content from generic templates that adds no original insight. YouTube says automated tools are fine only if the result still shows the creator’s own creative, educational, or entertainment value. A faceless page is not itself named as a ban. A page of near-duplicate AI templates is named as ineligible.

TikTok’s Creator Rewards rules reject unoriginal or minimally edited video, slide-style low-quality video, and accounts that hit five video violations in 30 days or five account violations.

Instagram’s Reels bonus counts only original content and excludes aggregator accounts. The help page does not define “aggregator.”

**Synthetic-media labels.** TikTok requires a label on realistic AI-generated images, audio, and video, and says it may remove unlabeled realistic AI content that breaks its integrity rules. Creators can label a post; TikTok also auto-labels some TikTok AI effects and uploads that carry C2PA Content Credentials. An auto label cannot be removed. Labeling unaltered content as AI is a terms violation and can lead to removal. YouTube requires disclosure when AI makes a real person appear to say or do something they did not do, alters a real event or place, generates a realistic scene that did not occur, or creates music that is the focus of the video. The label can sit on the player or in the description. YouTube says disclosure alone does not limit the audience or the ability to earn. It also says creators who consistently fail to disclose can have a label applied for them, content removed, or Partner Program access suspended. YouTube may auto-label its own GenAI tools, C2PA metadata, and content its systems detect. Meta says it labels detected or disclosed AI content on Instagram as “AI Info,” including on Reels. This pass did not find an Instagram rule that the label itself stops a bonus.

**Offers that a 7-day read will misread.** A TikTok reward video under one minute never enters the program, no matter the 7-day views. A sponsored or affiliate TikTok does not qualify for rewards. An Instagram branded Reel does not earn the bonus, and bonus-eligible pieces keep earning until day 28, so day 7 is mid-window. YouTube’s Shorts money is a monthly pool after music licensing, not a 7-day RPM. From February 1, 2027, a month of Shorts pool pay also depends on a 90-day view count.

## Conflicts

- TikTok cash-out: a Support FAQ says payment on the 15th if the amount meets **$10** or the local equivalent. The US Creator Rewards terms, in the search snippet of the legal page, say **$50**, and that amounts under the threshold may be treated as expired at TikTok’s discretion. The Brazil terms snippet and the EEA German terms snippet also say **$50**. The German snippet then says the payable amount is transferred even when it is under the minimum, as shown on the balance dashboard. The US legal page and the Creator Academy page timed out on full fetch, so the $50 and Academy eligibility text rest on search snippets of those URLs. The eight-country list and the 10,000 / 100,000 thresholds appear in more than one snippet, including the August 18, 2026 eligibility article.
- Instagram Reels bonus geography: the help center says bonus invitations require location and tax status in Japan, South Korea, or the United States. The creators bonuses page says the Reels bonus is only available in the United States and South Korea. The same help page lists photo-and-carousel view floors for the United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, New Zealand, Nordics, Ireland, and South Africa, which the invitation sentence does not include.
- Instagram “ads on Reels” versus “no general payout”: the business help page says eligible creators can share in that ad revenue. The creator monetization list does not include that product, and the profile-ad page still calls profile and Reels ad monetization a limited test. A 2023 Meta post and a 2025 secondary report (Lindsey Gamble, citing a Meta comment to Adweek about a skippable-ad test) describe tests that did not establish a general creator share. The official pages do not publish a percentage either way.
- Affiliate “what converts”: vendor and Reddit figures point at comment-to-DM over link-in-bio. None has a method this lab can reuse. They conflict with each other on the metric (click-through, newsletter signup, one-off Amazon sales).

## Inferences

- Inference: a 7-day window can compare view counts, and it can compare clicks and on-site actions if the operator’s redirect and offer page record them per video. It cannot see TikTok’s following-month settlement, YouTube’s monthly pool and music split, Instagram bonus earnings from day 8 through day 28, affiliate returns, or a later channel-level demonetization.
- Inference: crowning a winner at day 7, after changing the hook, the topic, the length, and the offer together, does not identify which factor moved views or clicks. That follows from Adobe’s and Kissmetrics’ warnings about early stopping and multiple metrics. It is not a result those sources measured on short video.
- Inference: early view velocity is a weak primary metric for this lab. The stated success measures are revenue and clicks. Views are a clue, and on TikTok and Instagram they can be high on videos that are ineligible for the platform payout.
- Inference: the three money paths should be scored separately. One video can be a Creator Rewards candidate only if it stays over one minute, unsponsored, and free of a contact-style bio. An affiliate or own-offer video should be judged on tracked clicks and confirmed revenue, and its views should not be read as program earnings.
- Inference: a faceless or AI-assisted video is not, by the sources above, banned for being faceless. The failure mode the policies name is mass-produced templates, scraped or minimally changed footage, undisclosed realistic synthetic media, and repeated violations. A 7-day view count will not show a later channel-level loss of monetization.
